How we help

Why Years 7 to 9 matter so much

  • GCSE is built on it

    GCSE Maths assumes everything from KS3: fractions, negative numbers, algebra, ratio. Gaps left now show up again in Year 10.

  • We follow their class

    We find out what they're doing at school and use the same methods as their teacher, so tutoring and lessons pull the same way.

  • Confidence first

    Lessons start with something they can already do, then build up in small steps, so confidence grows along with the maths.

  • You see the progress

    Homework comes with marks and feedback in the student area, so you can see what's been done and how it went.

The course

What we cover

Everything in the KS3 national curriculum, in the order your child's school teaches it (many schools follow the White Rose Maths scheme). That includes:

  • Number: place value, negative numbers, fractions, decimals and percentages, primes and factors
  • Algebra: notation, simplifying, equations, sequences and graphs
  • Ratio and proportion, including scale, maps and percentage change
  • Geometry and measures: angles, area, volume and constructions
  • Probability and statistics
  • Problem solving and explaining answers, which GCSE rewards

Ask us a question
Is Year 7 too early for a tutor?

Not if there's a gap to close or confidence to build. KS3 is a good time to fix things, before the pressure of GCSE. Year 8 and 9 are also good times to start, once topics like algebra and ratio begin to feel harder.

Do you follow what they're doing at school?

Yes. On the free intro we ask what they're doing in class, and lessons build on that using the same methods as their teacher. That way tutoring makes school work stick, instead of running a separate course.

How much does KS3 tutoring cost?

£25 an hour, paid per lesson with no long-term contract. The first session is a free 20 to 30 minute introductory call.

Are lessons online?

Yes. All lessons are one-to-one over Zoom, so you can be anywhere in the UK.
